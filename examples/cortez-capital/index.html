The ownership question

Read the business.
Before rewriting it.

What is worth preserving?
What could be stronger?

Start with the people, relationships and day-to-day work behind a business. Understand what already works before deciding what should change.

This concept journal explores that question through three operating situations. It describes a way of thinking, not a record of investments.

Explore three illustrative ownership perspectives. These scenarios describe the concept’s thinking, not actual portfolio holdings or investment opportunities.

01Dependability.The everyday / Essential services

Illustrative focus / 01

Essential services

Businesses people depend on, with work that has a clear purpose in the everyday.

The imagined lens
Consistency of service and the people who deliver it.
The operating question
How could better scheduling and clearer handoffs support the team?
A day in the operating conversation

Where does a service request arrive? Who assigns the work? What tells the next person that the job is complete?

Example workstream: request → assignment → completion review.

Example operating discussion, not investment criteria, due diligence, valuation or advice.

02Connection.The wider view / Regional operators

Illustrative focus / 02

Regional operators

Locally rooted businesses whose relationships and practical knowledge connect a wider region.

The imagined lens
Local understanding, with a consistent experience across locations.
The operating question
What information should move more clearly between teams?
A cross-location working note

Which updates does each location need? Who owns a correction? Where should exceptions be visible rather than buried in a message thread?

Example workstream: local update → shared view → exception review.

Example operating discussion, not investment criteria, due diligence, valuation or advice.

03Continuity.The next chapter / Founder transitions

Illustrative focus / 03

Founder transitions

A business story moving into a new chapter, with care for the knowledge and relationships already built.

The imagined lens
Continuity of purpose and a thoughtful transfer of knowledge.
The operating question
What needs to be documented so the next team can build on the foundation?
A continuity working note

Which recurring tasks depend on one person? Where are procedures documented? Who can explain the next step when that person is away?

Example workstream: recurring task → documented procedure → named owner.

Example operating discussion, not investment criteria, due diligence, valuation or advice.

The approach

Three habits.
One considered handover.

Understand how work arrives, who owns the next step and what needs to be handed over.

I

Holding

Patient ownership.

A longer horizon creates room to understand the business, respect its history, and consider decisions in context.

O

Building

Operating discipline.

Useful systems, clear responsibilities, and steady communication make a stronger foundation for the everyday.

G

Growing

Thoughtful progress.

Strengthen what makes a business valuable without losing the character and relationships that brought it here.

Stewardship

Leave a clearer
handover.

An owner-operator perspective begins with listening. Understand the people, the knowledge, and the relationships before deciding what should change.

Preserve the knowledge. Clarify the responsibilities. Make the next handoff easier.
